§1

Problem statement

Generative AI introduces governance and cybersecurity risks spanning data protection, third-party accountability, human oversight, information security, and incident management — risks that organizations must address throughout the AI lifecycle (AI Verify Foundation & Infocomm Media Development Authority [IMDA], 2024; Autio et al., 2024).

There is no structured baseline within the participating SME advisory context for determining the extent to which Singapore SMEs consider these risks, the barriers affecting their ability to address them, and the practical support they need.

This project addresses that evidence gap through an exploratory pilot: a convenience sample of five to eight SMEs recruited through the SAIA AI Discovery Clinic, with SCCCI included as an additional recruitment partner if formally confirmed.

Guiding research question: What minimum cybersecurity and governance controls should an SME implement before employees use generative AI for business work?

§2

About this capstone

This pilot is the applied component of the IT595 Master's Capstone in Cybersecurity Management at Purdue Global University, advised by Dr Ash Luitel. It designs and evaluates a practical AI security baseline for SMEs — not a restatement of any single framework, but an original, SME-oriented assessment tool and roadmap.

The capstone will examine a convenience sample of five to eight Singapore SMEs recruited through the SAIA AI Discovery Clinic, with SCCCI included as an additional recruitment partner if formally confirmed. Rather than modeling a single representative organization, the study will use 12 supplemental governance-and-safety questions to explore how participating SMEs consider AI-related risks, what barriers affect their ability to address those risks, and what practical support they need. The questions are informed by authoritative international and Singapore guidance, and the findings will be reported as an exploratory snapshot rather than as a readiness certification or representation of all Singapore SMEs. Five authoritative sources inform the question set, each given a distinct role rather than treated as interchangeable:

NIST AI RMF

The overarching risk-management structure — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.

NIST CSF 2.0

A common cybersecurity outcome language across organizations of any size.

ISO/IEC 42001

The formal, certifiable management-system anchor.

OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10

The application-security threat lens for LLM-powered systems.

AI Verify / IMDA

Singapore's voluntary testing framework and Model AI Governance guidance for generative and agentic AI — practical process checks and evidence expectations.

§3

Ten risk domains, scored on a 0–3 maturity scale

Each SME is assessed against ten domains, translating NIST, ISO, OWASP, and AI Verify/IMDA guidance into questions an advisor can ask without specialist training. A domain isn't scored on a single answer — it's coded from four cumulative evidence checks: does the practice exist, is it documented, is it implemented, and is it reviewed.

01

AI use context

AI tools and use cases are identified, recorded, assigned an owner, and kept current.

02

Accountability & governance

Decision rights, ownership, approval, escalation, and pre-approval risk review are assigned and used.

03

Data protection

Rules govern what information employees may enter into AI tools and how AI-generated output is handled.

04

Third-party management

AI vendors and tools receive risk-based security, privacy, and contractual review before and during use.

05

Access & integration security

Access to AI tools and connected systems uses individual identity, least privilege, and timely offboarding.

06

Human oversight

Risk-based human review, override, and escalation are defined for consequential AI outputs or actions.

07

Testing & assurance

AI use cases are tested against documented fitness, safety, and reliability criteria before use and after change.

08

Monitoring & change

Operational AI use is monitored with assigned metrics, logs, thresholds, and change triggers.

09

Incident response

AI-related events can be identified, contained, escalated, reported, and used to improve controls.

10

People & policy

Employees receive role-relevant AI policy, guidance, and training, kept current as risks change.

Barriers and support is asked of every SME but isn't scored — it's the qualitative question that explains why a domain scored the way it did, and what practical support (templates, training, assessments, specialist advice) would move it forward.
ScoreMaturityWhat it means
0AbsentThe practice is absent or has not been considered.
1Informal / ad hocA practice exists, but it's informal and dependent on individuals.
2Documented & implementedDocumented and operating, but not routinely monitored or reviewed.
3Managed & reviewedDocumented, implemented, monitored, and periodically reviewed.

§4

Interview process

Every SME goes through the same six steps, so scoring stays consistent across a cohort that otherwise looks nothing alike.

01

Prepare

Review the question, the control it measures, the four evidence checks, and the expected evidence before the interview.

02

Interview

Ask each question conversationally and probe for examples. The narrative is the evidence — the four checks are never read out as a checklist.

03

Code evidence

After listening, code Practice, Documented, Implemented, and Reviewed as Yes, No, or Unknown.

04

Record basis

Select the strongest evidence basis and confidence level. No confidential documents are requested or stored.

05

Review score

The 0–3 maturity score calculates automatically. A blank or Unknown required check returns Incomplete or Needs review, rather than a silent zero.

06

Analyze

SME- and domain-level patterns come from the Results view; barrier and support themes come from every SME's qualitative question.

§5

Capstone milestones

Four milestones move the project from scope and research, through prototype design, to scenario validation and final delivery.

M1

Initiation, research & requirements Units 2/3

Confirm scope and success measures, review NIST, ISO, OWASP, and AI Verify/IMDA guidance, build the use-case and threat inventory, and draft the initial control taxonomy.

M2

Baseline & assessment prototype Units 4/5

Define minimum control statements, design assessment questions and evidence prompts, build the scoring and decision model, and assemble the Version 1.0 prototype toolkit.

M3

Scenario validation & refinement Units 6/7

Test the prototype against three representative use cases — an employee assistant, a customer chatbot, and a limited workflow agent — then evaluate usability, coverage, and revise.

M4

Finalization, roadmap & capstone delivery Units 8/9

Synthesize findings, finalize a 0–30/31–60/61–90-day remediation roadmap, complete the toolkit, and deliver the final capstone report.

Milestones are mapped to IT595 course units; calendar dates against the 31 Oct target will be added once the term schedule is confirmed.

§8

Results

Fieldwork is underway; aggregate results will be published here once scoring is complete.

SME-level: completed scores, average score, % of maximum, and a maturity label (Not assessed until an SME's interview is coded).

Domain-level: average score across the cohort for each of the ten domains, plus a distribution across 0, 1, and 2–3.

Barriers & support: a qualitative synthesis of what SMEs said was making governance and cybersecurity difficult, and what support they asked for.

Results are reported in aggregate. Individual SMEs are not ranked or identified publicly.
